Project overview

What we're building

A structured pruning + activation healing pipeline (TIM-MGDT) that identifies which transformer layers and MLP widths are elastic, critical, or reasoning-specific. The current focus is Mistral-7B compression that survives GSM8K rather than merely preserving C4 perplexity.

Why this matters

Phase 16 showed the central failure mode: 20% hardware-aligned width pruning kept C4 perplexity in a plausible range but collapsed 5-shot GSM8K from 38.7% to about 5%. That is a useful negative result because it separates general language retention from reasoning-circuit retention.

Where we are today

Phase 18B planning. Phase 17 contrastive sparing and Phase 18 non-uniform masking both failed 50-sample GSM8K smoke tests. The next experiment should be a true structural, sensitivity-guided sparsity sweep at 5%, 10%, 15%, and 20% before any full GSM8K run.

The research question

What is the highest structural MLP-width sparsity that preserves Mistral-7B's 5-shot GSM8K reasoning, and which layer-sensitivity rule explains that boundary?

Phase 15: Incision Boundary Healing for Depth-Pruned LLMs

While recent literature (e.g., Gromov et al., ShortGPT, LaCo) has established that Layer Dropping achieves true hardware compression, the standard practice of applying global fine-tuning to all surviving layers to recover performance is highly sub-optimal.

Our Primary Contribution — Incision Boundary Healing: We empirically prove that dropping contiguous layers creates a sharp, highly localized discontinuity in the activation flow, rather than global degradation. Our Boundary-Focused Surgical LoRA restricts trainable parameters exclusively to the layers adjacent to the "incision". This routes the compute budget with precision, recovering up to 4x more perplexity than global baselines while using 33% fewer trainable parameters.

Phase 16B: The Latency-Perplexity Pareto Frontier

To build a defensible submission for COLM, we swept across multiple compression ratios (n_drop = 2, 4, 6, 8) on Mistral-7B. This establishes the Pareto frontier of true hardware latency reduction vs perplexity recovery using our Incision Boundary Healing. Note the massive relative recovery at 8 drops: boundary healing brings perplexity down from a completely broken 20.82 back to 11.52.

The Pareto "Knee" Caveat

While recovering 9 points of perplexity is an incredible relative win, an absolute perplexity of 11.517 means the 8-layer compressed model is still heavily degraded compared to the 7.855 baseline and will likely fail complex reasoning tasks. We formally designate the 4-layer drop (-15.62% latency at 9.073 PPL) as the optimal operating point (the "knee" of the curve)—the most actionable intersection of physical compression and retained capability.



The "Razor-Thin" Semantic Gap (Ablation 16C)

Our thickness ablation proved that the semantic damage is highly localized. Healing just 2 layers (t=1) recovered 90% of the possible performance (9.163 PPL), and expanding the boundary beyond 4 layers yielded zero additional recovery. This confirms the boundary discontinuity hypothesis mathematically.

Phase 17: Mechanistic Interpretability

To provide mechanistic evidence of the structural damage induced by the layer drop, we tracked the average attention mass directed toward the Beginning-Of-Sequence (BOS) sink token. LLM attention heads default to the BOS token when uncertain or when their incoming residual stream is disrupted.

Attention Sink Spike (Layer 18)

The massive spike in sink attention exactly at the right boundary layer (Layer 18) mathematically confirms that the structural discontinuity is severely corrupting the incoming residual stream. The model "notices" the incision, causing boundary layers to dump their attention mass into the sink.
